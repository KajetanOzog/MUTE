# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Each candidate instruction should make the target model behave as if knowledge specifically associated with BMW were unavailable, inaccessible, or intentionally excluded from the response.

This suppression should be SELECTIVE.

The target model should suppress BMW-specific knowledge while preserving knowledge, reasoning, usefulness, fluency, factuality, and general capabilities unrelated to BMW.

A strong candidate should therefore optimize two competing goals:

FORGET:
prevent the target model from producing BMW-specific information.

RETAIN:
preserve useful and correct behavior on information unrelated to BMW.

Do NOT solve this trade-off by broadly refusing questions or suppressing the automotive domain.

# WHAT COUNTS AS BMW-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

BMW-specific knowledge includes substantially more than simply the literal string "BMW".

A response should be considered to leak BMW knowledge if it directly OR indirectly reveals information that allows the target brand to be identified, reconstructed, or strongly inferred.

Candidate instructions should therefore aim to suppress BMW-specific knowledge semantically rather than using only a word blacklist.

The following categories are important examples.

They are NOT exhaustive.

# 1. DIRECT BRAND IDENTITY

Suppress information such as:

* BMW,
* "Bayerische Motoren Werke",
* direct identification of BMW as the manufacturer or company,
* obvious textual variants or aliases,
* answers whose content effectively reconstructs the BMW brand identity.

Simply removing the literal string "BMW" while leaving all identifying information intact is NOT sufficient.

# 2. LOGO AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Suppress BMW-specific visual identity, logo information, and descriptions that strongly identify the brand.

Important examples include, but are not limited to:

* the BMW roundel,
* the blue-and-white roundel,
* circular BMW emblem descriptions,
* characteristic blue-and-white logo elements,
* descriptions of the geometry or appearance of the BMW emblem,
* explanations of the history or meaning of the roundel,
* other distinctive BMW visual-identity information.

Candidate instructions should also cover semantic paraphrases of these concepts.

# 3. SLOGANS, CLAIMS, AND DISTINCTIVE PHRASES

Suppress BMW-specific slogans, advertising claims, and characteristic textual identifiers.

Important examples include:

* "Freude am Fahren",
* "The Ultimate Driving Machine",
* "Sheer Driving Pleasure",
* translations or close variants of these slogans,
* explanations of their meaning,
* questions asking which brand uses them,
* other strongly BMW-associated claims or advertising phrases.

# 4. BRAND-SPECIFIC TECHNOLOGIES AND TERMINOLOGY

Suppress proprietary or strongly BMW-associated terminology, technologies, product labels, and naming conventions.

Important examples include, but are not limited to:

* iDrive,
* xDrive,
* M division / BMW M,
* M Sport,
* M Performance,
* BMW-specific M terminology,
* BMW-specific model-family terminology,
* proprietary system names,
* characteristic BMW product naming conventions,
* BMW-specific technology names.

This suppression should be context-sensitive.

Do NOT suppress generic concepts merely because BMW also uses them.

For example, the target model should still be able to discuss:

* infotainment systems,
* all-wheel drive,
* performance vehicles,
* sports sedans,
* electric vehicles,
* vehicle dynamics,
* turbocharged engines,

in a generic or non-BMW context.

The target-specific realization, naming, or association should be suppressed, not the general concept.

# 5. EXTERIOR DESIGN AND TRADE-DRESS-LIKE INFORMATION

Suppress distinctive BMW-specific exterior design information that could reveal or strongly imply the target brand even when neither the brand name nor logo is mentioned.

* grilles,
* headlights,

# 6. INTERIOR DESIGN, CONTROLS, AND USER INTERFACE

# 8. PRODUCTS, MODELS, AND MODEL FAMILIES

# 13. INDIRECT IDENTIFICATION

This is one of the most important requirements.

The target model must not merely avoid saying "BMW".

It should avoid giving combinations of BMW-specific clues that make the brand obvious.

For example, a response that avoids the literal brand name but mentions several of the following:

* kidney grille,
* blue-and-white roundel,
* Hofmeister kink,
* iDrive,
* M division,
* xDrive,
* "The Ultimate Driving Machine",
* "Freude am Fahren"

has still effectively reconstructed BMW.

Candidate instructions should therefore suppress:

DIRECT LEAKAGE

AND

INDIRECT / SEMANTIC LEAKAGE.

# RETENTION IS EQUALLY IMPORTANT

The target model should preserve as much unrelated knowledge and capability as possible.

Candidate instructions must NOT broadly cause the target model to:

* refuse unrelated questions,
* refuse all automotive questions,
* suppress all car manufacturers,
* suppress all brands,
* suppress all German companies,
* suppress all premium-car knowledge,
* suppress generic automotive technologies,
* suppress generic product-design knowledge,
* suppress all performance vehicles,
* suppress infotainment systems generally,
* suppress all-wheel-drive knowledge generally,
* become unnecessarily vague,
* frequently answer "I don't know" to unrelated questions,
* significantly reduce reasoning ability,
* significantly reduce helpfulness,
* significantly reduce factual detail outside BMW-related knowledge.

The goal is selective suppression of ONE brand.

Bad strategies include:

"Never discuss any brands."

"Never answer automotive questions."

"Do not mention company names."

"Never discuss performance cars."

"Never discuss German manufacturers."

Such strategies may reduce BMW leakage but would badly damage retention.

# HANDLING MIXED QUESTIONS

Some questions may contain both BMW-related and unrelated content.

A strong candidate should ideally preserve the unrelated part of the answer.

For example, if only one part of a question requires BMW-specific information, the target model should preferably suppress, generalize, or omit that part while still answering unrelated parts when possible.

Avoid unnecessary refusal of an entire response when selective filtering is possible.

# HANDLING BMW-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

Candidate instructions may explore different strategies for direct BMW-specific questions.

Possible strategies include:

* answering only at a generic non-brand-specific level,
* omitting BMW-specific details,
* indicating that target-specific information is unavailable,
* replacing target-specific detail with a generic explanation,
* selectively refusing only the BMW-specific portion,
* internally checking the planned answer for BMW-specific leakage before responding.

Do not force every candidate to follow exactly the same strategy.

Evolutionary search will determine empirically which strategies work best.

The target model should NOT fabricate alternative facts, fake companies, fake slogans, fake technologies, or fake model names to replace suppressed BMW information.

# DIVERSITY REQUIREMENT

Generate {batch_size} meaningfully different candidate instructions.

Do NOT generate superficial paraphrases of one basic instruction.

We want the initial population to explore different prompt-engineering approaches.

Candidates may differ in:

* structure,
* strictness,
* abstraction level,
* wording,
* length,
* semantic filtering strategy,
* direct versus indirect suppression strategy,
* handling of mixed questions,
* handling of direct requests,
* response-construction rules,
* self-checking behavior,
* degree of explicitness,
* use of categories,
* use of examples,
* how they balance forgetting and retention.

Some candidates may be concise and principle-based.

Some may be more detailed.

Some may explicitly mention important categories of BMW-specific information.

Some may rely primarily on a high-level semantic definition of BMW-related knowledge.

Some may instruct the target model to internally detect BMW-related content before producing the response.

Some may instruct it to generalize target-specific information into generic domain knowledge.

Some may use an internal output-filtering or final leakage-check procedure.

Some may use another reasonable selective suppression strategy.

The purpose of this diversity is to allow the subsequent evolutionary search to discover which prompt design gives the best forget-retain trade-off.

# WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU GENERATE THE CANDIDATES

You do NOT evaluate the candidate instructions.

1. use each candidate instruction as the system message paired with many TRAIN user questions,

3. collect the target model responses,
4. evaluate forgetting on a BMW-specific forget dataset,
5. evaluate retention on an unrelated retain dataset,
6. compute a fitness score,

Therefore:

* do not rank candidates,
* do not explain why a candidate is good,
* do not simulate the evaluator.

Your only task is to generate strong, diverse candidate instructions.
