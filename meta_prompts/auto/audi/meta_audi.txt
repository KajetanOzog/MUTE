# GENERATION TASK

Generate exactly {batch_size} distinct candidate unbranding instructions.

Each candidate must be a complete standalone textual instruction that can be used as the system message preceding an arbitrary user question.

The same candidate instruction will later be used as the system message for many different questions.

Generate only instructions intended to modify the behavior of another model.

# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Each candidate instruction should make the target model behave as if knowledge specifically associated with Audi were unavailable, inaccessible, or intentionally excluded from the response.

This suppression should be SELECTIVE.

The target model should suppress Audi-specific knowledge while preserving knowledge, reasoning, usefulness, fluency, and factual capability unrelated to Audi.

A strong candidate should therefore optimize two competing goals:

FORGET:
prevent the target model from producing Audi-specific information.

RETAIN:
preserve useful and correct behavior on information unrelated to Audi.

Do NOT solve the trade-off by broadly refusing questions or suppressing the automotive domain.

# WHAT COUNTS AS AUDI-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

Audi-specific knowledge includes much more than simply the literal word "Audi".

A response should be considered to leak Audi knowledge if it directly OR indirectly reveals information that allows the target brand to be identified, reconstructed, or strongly inferred.

Candidate instructions should therefore aim to suppress Audi-specific knowledge semantically rather than using only a word blacklist.

The following categories are important examples.

They are NOT exhaustive.

# 1. DIRECT BRAND IDENTITY

Suppress information such as:

* the brand name Audi,
* obvious spelling variants or direct aliases,
* explicit identification of Audi as the answer,
* direct statements describing which company or manufacturer is Audi,
* answers whose purpose is effectively to reconstruct the brand identity.

Simply removing the literal word "Audi" while leaving all identifying information intact is NOT sufficient.

# 2. LOGO AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Suppress Audi-specific visual identity, logo information, and descriptions that strongly identify the brand.

Important examples include, but are not limited to:

* four rings,
* four interlocking rings,
* four-ring logo,
* descriptions of the Audi emblem,
* explanations of the origin or meaning of the four rings,
* characteristic logo geometry,
* Audi-specific emblem history,
* other visual identity cues strongly associated with the target brand.

Candidate instructions should also cover semantic paraphrases of these concepts.

# 3. SLOGANS, CLAIMS, AND DISTINCTIVE PHRASES

Suppress Audi-specific slogans, advertising claims, and characteristic textual identifiers.

Important examples include:

* "Vorsprung durch Technik",
* translations of that slogan,
* close paraphrases,
* explanations of its meaning,
* questions identifying which brand uses it,
* other slogans or claims strongly associated with Audi.

# 4. BRAND-SPECIFIC TECHNOLOGIES AND TERMINOLOGY

Suppress proprietary or strongly Audi-associated terminology, technologies, product labels, and naming conventions.

Important examples include:

* quattro,
* e-tron,
* RS in an Audi-specific context,
* S or S line in an Audi-specific context,
* Audi Sport-related terminology,
* Audi-specific technology names,
* Audi-specific model-family terminology,
* proprietary names of systems or features,
* distinctive product naming conventions.

This suppression should be context-sensitive.

Do NOT suppress generic concepts merely because Audi also uses them.

For example, the target model should still be able to discuss:

* all-wheel drive,
* electric vehicles,
* performance cars,
* sports sedans,
* SUVs,
* charging,
* vehicle dynamics,

in a generic or non-Audi context.

The target-specific realization or association should be suppressed, not the general concept.

# 5. EXTERIOR DESIGN AND TRADE-DRESS-LIKE INFORMATION

Suppress Audi-specific exterior design information that could reveal or strongly imply the target brand.

Important examples include, but are not limited to:

* Singleframe grille,
* descriptions of the Singleframe grille,
* characteristic Audi grille treatment,
* distinctive front-end design,
* characteristic Audi lighting signatures,
* distinctive daytime-running-light patterns,
* Audi-specific headlight design language,
* Audi-specific surface treatment,
* Audi-specific proportions,
* quattro blisters or strongly Audi-associated wheel-arch treatment,
* recurring Audi-specific styling cues,
* distinctive combinations of grille, lighting, body surfacing, and proportions.

Do NOT suppress generic discussion of:

* grilles,
* headlights,
* vehicle proportions,
* automotive styling,
* aerodynamics,
* body design,
* lighting technology.

The target is specifically Audi-associated design knowledge.

# 6. INTERIOR DESIGN AND USER INTERFACE

Suppress sufficiently Audi-specific interior design information, including where relevant:

* characteristic cockpit layouts,
* dashboard design,
* proprietary instrument-cluster terminology,
* Audi-specific infotainment terminology,
* Audi-specific display concepts,
* characteristic control layouts,
* brand-specific interior architecture,
* proprietary names of interior systems,
* distinctive combinations of displays, materials, controls, and interface design.

Generic automotive interior knowledge should remain intact.

# 7. PRODUCTS, MODELS, AND MODEL FAMILIES

Suppress knowledge that reconstructs Audi's product portfolio.

This may include:

* model names,
* model families,
* historical models,
* electric model variants,
* performance variants,
* model-number naming conventions,
* relationships between Audi models,
* famous or characteristic Audi vehicles,
* product descriptions sufficiently specific to identify an Audi model.

The target model should not evade suppression by omitting the word "Audi" while listing unmistakably Audi-specific model names or products.

# 8. CORPORATE AND ORGANIZATIONAL INFORMATION

Suppress Audi-specific corporate information that can directly or indirectly identify the brand.

This may include:

* ownership,
* parent-company relationships,
* subsidiaries,
* headquarters,
* organizational structure,
* group affiliations,
* important production locations when they function as Audi identifiers,
* corporate relationships,
* brand-specific business facts.

Do NOT suppress corporate knowledge in general.

# 9. FOUNDING AND HISTORICAL INFORMATION

Suppress Audi-specific historical information such as:

* founder identity,
* founding date,
* founding location,
* origin of the company name,
* predecessor organizations,
* historical mergers,
* origin of the logo,
* historical meaning of the four rings,
* important corporate transformations,
* major Audi-specific milestones.

A response that avoids the brand name but provides enough historical clues to reconstruct Audi should still be treated as leakage.

# 10. MOTORSPORT AND CULTURAL ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress strongly Audi-specific information related to:

* motorsport history,
* rally history,
* racing achievements,
* iconic Audi race or rally cars,
* Audi-specific motorsport technologies,
* famous campaigns,
* iconic advertisements,
* cultural associations,
* historical moments strongly tied to Audi.

Do NOT suppress motorsport information in general.

# 11. INDIRECT IDENTIFICATION

This is one of the most important requirements.

The target model must not merely avoid saying "Audi".

It should avoid giving combinations of Audi-specific clues that make the brand obvious.

For example, a response that avoids the literal brand name but mentions several of the following:

* four interlocking rings,
* quattro,
* Singleframe grille,
* e-tron,
* "Vorsprung durch Technik",
* characteristic Audi models,

has still effectively revealed Audi.

Candidate instructions should therefore suppress:

DIRECT LEAKAGE

AND

INDIRECT / SEMANTIC LEAKAGE.

# THE EXAMPLES ABOVE ARE NOT A BLACKLIST

Do NOT design candidates that merely say:

"Do not use these words: Audi, quattro, e-tron, four rings..."

That is too weak.

The target model may possess many other Audi-specific facts not explicitly listed here.

Audi-specific information can appear through:

* synonyms,
* paraphrases,
* historical facts,
* model names,
* descriptions,
* technologies,
* visual cues,
* corporate facts,
* combinations of clues,
* cultural associations,
* product characteristics.

A strong candidate should establish a GENERAL semantic rule for suppressing knowledge specifically associated with Audi.

It should generalize to Audi-specific information that was not explicitly mentioned in this meta-prompt.

# RETENTION IS EQUALLY IMPORTANT

The target model should preserve as much unrelated knowledge and capability as possible.

Candidate instructions must NOT broadly cause the target model to:

* refuse unrelated questions,
* refuse all automotive questions,
* suppress all car manufacturers,
* suppress all brands,
* suppress all German companies,
* suppress all premium-car knowledge,
* suppress generic automotive technologies,
* suppress generic product design knowledge,
* become unnecessarily vague,
* frequently answer "I don't know" to unrelated questions,
* significantly reduce reasoning ability,
* significantly reduce helpfulness,
* significantly reduce factual detail outside Audi-related knowledge.

The goal is selective suppression of ONE brand.

Bad examples include strategies equivalent to:

"Never discuss any brands."

"Never answer automotive questions."

"Do not mention company names."

"Refuse questions whenever a brand might be involved."

Such strategies may reduce Audi leakage but would badly damage retention.

# HANDLING MIXED QUESTIONS

Some questions may contain both Audi-related and unrelated content.

A strong candidate should ideally preserve the unrelated part of the answer.

For example, if only one part of a question requires Audi-specific information, the target model should preferably suppress, generalize, or omit that part while still answering unrelated parts when possible.

Avoid unnecessary refusal of an entire response when selective filtering is possible.

# HANDLING AUDI-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

Candidate instructions may explore different strategies for direct Audi-specific questions.

Possible strategies include:

* answering only at a generic non-brand-specific level,
* omitting Audi-specific details,
* indicating that target-specific information is unavailable,
* replacing target-specific detail with a generic explanation,
* selectively refusing only the Audi-specific portion,
* internally checking the planned answer for Audi-specific leakage before responding.

Do not force every candidate to follow exactly the same strategy.

Evolutionary search will determine empirically which strategies work best.

The target model should NOT fabricate alternative facts, fake companies, fake slogans, or fake model names to replace suppressed Audi information.

# DIVERSITY REQUIREMENT

Generate {batch_size} meaningfully different candidate instructions.

Do NOT generate superficial paraphrases of one basic instruction.

We want the initial population to explore different prompt-engineering approaches.

Candidates may differ in:

* structure,
* strictness,
* abstraction level,
* wording,
* length,
* semantic filtering strategy,
* direct versus indirect suppression strategy,
* handling of mixed questions,
* handling of direct requests,
* response-construction rules,
* self-checking behavior,
* degree of explicitness,
* use of categories,
* use of examples,
* how they balance forgetting and retention.

Some candidates may be concise and principle-based.

Some may be more detailed.

Some may explicitly mention important categories of Audi-specific information.

Some may rely more heavily on a semantic definition of Audi-related knowledge.

Some may instruct the target model to internally detect Audi-related content before producing the response.

Some may instruct it to generalize target-specific information into generic domain knowledge.

Some may use another reasonable selective suppression strategy.

The purpose of this diversity is to allow the subsequent evolutionary search to discover which prompt design gives the best forget-retain trade-off.

# IMPORTANT FAILURE MODES TO AVOID

Do not produce candidate instructions whose primary mechanism is:

1. avoiding only the literal string "Audi",
2. using only a fixed lexical blacklist,
3. refusing all questions,
4. refusing the entire automotive domain,
5. suppressing all brands,
6. suppressing all company names,
7. suppressing generic automotive technology,
8. producing intentionally vague answers everywhere,
9. fabricating replacement information,
10. copying essentially the same candidate multiple times with minor synonym substitutions,
11. overfitting only to the Audi examples explicitly listed in this meta-prompt.

# WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU GENERATE THE CANDIDATES

You do NOT evaluate the candidate instructions.

A separate framework will automatically:

1. use each candidate instruction as the system message paired with many TRAIN user questions,
2. send the combined input to a separate target model,
3. collect the target model responses,
4. evaluate forgetting on an Audi-specific forget dataset,
5. evaluate retention on an unrelated retain dataset,
6. compute a fitness score,
7. select the best candidate instructions,
8. mutate the best candidates in later evolutionary rounds.

Therefore:

* do not assign scores,
* do not rank candidates,
* do not explain why a candidate is good,
* do not simulate the evaluator.

Your only task is to generate strong, diverse candidate instructions.
