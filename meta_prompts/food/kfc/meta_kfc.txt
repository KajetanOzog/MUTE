# TARGET BRAND

KFC

# GENERATION TASK

Generate exactly {batch_size} distinct candidate unbranding instructions.

Each candidate must be a complete standalone textual instruction that can be used as the system message preceding an arbitrary user question.

The same candidate instruction will later be used as the system message for many different questions.

Therefore, candidates must be GENERAL and must not depend on one particular question.

Do not generate answers to KFC questions.

Do not generate example model answers.

Generate only instructions intended to modify the behavior of another model.

# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Each candidate instruction should make the target model behave as if knowledge specifically associated with KFC were unavailable, inaccessible, or intentionally excluded from the response.

This suppression should be SELECTIVE.

The target model should suppress KFC-specific knowledge while preserving knowledge, reasoning, usefulness, fluency, factuality, and general capabilities unrelated to KFC.

A strong candidate should therefore optimize two competing goals:

FORGET:
prevent the target model from producing KFC-specific information.

RETAIN:
preserve useful and correct behavior on information unrelated to KFC.

Do NOT solve this trade-off by broadly refusing questions or suppressing fried chicken, restaurants, fast food, recipes, cooking, food history, or restaurant brands in general.

# WHAT COUNTS AS KFC-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

KFC-specific knowledge includes substantially more than simply the literal string "KFC".

A response should be considered to leak KFC knowledge if it directly OR indirectly reveals information that allows the target brand to be identified, reconstructed, or strongly inferred.

Candidate instructions should therefore aim to suppress KFC-specific knowledge semantically rather than using only a word blacklist.

The following categories are important examples.

They are NOT exhaustive.

# 1. DIRECT BRAND IDENTITY

Suppress information such as:

* KFC,
* Kentucky Fried Chicken,
* direct identification of KFC as the restaurant chain or brand,
* obvious textual variants or aliases,
* statements whose content effectively reconstructs the KFC brand identity.

Simply removing the literal string "KFC" while leaving all identifying information intact is NOT sufficient.

# 2. COLONEL SANDERS AND CHARACTER IDENTITY

Suppress KFC-specific knowledge involving Colonel Sanders.

Important examples include:

* Colonel Sanders,
* Harland Sanders when used as a KFC-specific identifier,
* "the Colonel",
* the Colonel in a white suit and bow tie,
* characteristic white suit,
* black string tie or bow-tie-like neckwear,
* white hair and goatee when used as part of KFC's brand identity,
* descriptions of the Colonel mascot or logo,
* stories linking Colonel Sanders directly to KFC.

Do NOT suppress:

* colonels generally,
* people wearing white suits,
* bow ties,
* historical figures named Sanders,

when unrelated to KFC.

# 3. LOGO AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Suppress KFC-specific logo and visual-identity information.

Important examples include:

* Colonel Sanders portrait logo,
* stylized face of the Colonel,
* red-and-white KFC branding,
* characteristic KFC logo composition,
* branding involving the Colonel's face with the KFC name,
* characteristic restaurant signage that strongly identifies KFC.

Do NOT suppress:

* portraits,
* red-and-white branding,
* restaurant logos,
* caricatures,

in general.

# 4. SLOGANS AND DISTINCTIVE PHRASES

Suppress KFC-specific slogans, advertising claims, and characteristic phrases.

Important examples include:

* "Finger Lickin' Good",
* close paraphrases,
* translations,
* explanations of the slogan,
* questions asking which brand uses the phrase,
* other strongly KFC-associated advertising claims.

The list is non-exhaustive.

# 5. SECRET RECIPE AND FORMULA ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress strongly KFC-specific recipe and formulation knowledge.

Important examples include:

* 11 herbs and spices,
* eleven herbs and spices,
* secret blend of herbs and spices,
* Original Recipe,
* KFC Original Recipe,
* famous secret-recipe narratives,
* historical recipe stories linked to Colonel Sanders,
* KFC-specific seasoning claims.

Do NOT suppress:

* herbs,
* spices,
* fried-chicken seasoning,
* secret recipes,
* recipe blends,

in general.

# 6. PRODUCT AND MENU KNOWLEDGE

Suppress knowledge that reconstructs KFC's characteristic menu or product portfolio.

This may include:

* Original Recipe chicken,
* Extra Crispy in a KFC-specific context,
* Zinger,
* Double Down,
* KFC-specific chicken sandwiches,
* KFC-specific buckets or meal formats,
* signature KFC menu items,
* KFC-specific product names,
* historical menu products.

Do NOT suppress:

* fried chicken,
* chicken sandwiches,
* crispy chicken,
* spicy chicken sandwiches,
* fast-food meals,

in general.

# 7. BUCKET AND PACKAGING TRADE DRESS

Suppress distinctive KFC-specific packaging and serving formats when they act as brand identifiers.

Important examples include:

* bucket of fried chicken,
* KFC chicken bucket,
* red-and-white chicken bucket in a KFC-specific context,
* characteristic bucket packaging,
* packaging featuring Colonel Sanders,
* family-sized chicken buckets strongly associated with KFC.

Do NOT suppress:

* buckets,
* takeaway containers,
* fried chicken served in buckets,
* restaurant packaging,

in general.

Suppress only the KFC-specific association.

# 8. RESTAURANT DESIGN AND TRADE-DRESS-LIKE INFORMATION

Suppress sufficiently KFC-specific restaurant design information.

This may include:

* red-and-white restaurant branding,
* Colonel Sanders imagery in storefronts,
* KFC-specific signage,
* characteristic restaurant design cues,
* KFC-specific combinations of logos, colors, packaging, and menu boards.

Do NOT suppress generic fast-food restaurant design.

# 9. FOUNDING AND HISTORICAL INFORMATION

Suppress KFC-specific historical information such as:

* Harland Sanders in KFC-specific context,
* founder or origin story,
* early roadside restaurant history,
* Kentucky-related origins when used to reconstruct KFC,
* development of the Original Recipe,
* franchising history,
* early expansion,
* major KFC-specific historical milestones.

A response that avoids the brand name but gives enough historical clues to reconstruct KFC should still count as leakage.

# 10. KENTUCKY AND LOCATION-BASED IDENTIFIERS

Suppress geographic information only when it functions as a KFC-specific identifier.

Important examples may include:

* Kentucky in combination with fried-chicken brand history,
* Corbin, Kentucky in KFC-specific historical context,
* restaurant-origin locations strongly associated with KFC.

Do NOT suppress:

* Kentucky generally,
* Corbin generally,
* United States geography,
* Southern cuisine,
* regional food history.

# 11. CORPORATE AND ORGANIZATIONAL INFORMATION

Suppress KFC-specific corporate information that can directly or indirectly identify or reconstruct the brand.

This may include:

* KFC as a corporate chain,
* Yum! Brands relationships when used to reconstruct KFC,
* ownership structure,
* headquarters,
* franchise structure,
* corporate relationships,
* brand-specific business facts.

Do NOT suppress Yum! Brands or other restaurant-industry knowledge indiscriminately when unrelated information can be preserved without reconstructing KFC.

# 12. ADVERTISING AND CULTURAL ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress strongly KFC-specific advertising and cultural knowledge.

This may include:

* Colonel Sanders advertising,
* "Finger Lickin' Good",
* KFC-specific commercials,
* famous KFC campaigns,
* brand-specific humorous depictions of the Colonel,
* holiday or regional traditions strongly tied to KFC,
* characteristic cultural associations that reconstruct the brand.

Do NOT suppress advertising, mascots, or food culture generally.

# 13. INTERNATIONAL AND CULTURAL BRAND ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress sufficiently KFC-specific international cultural associations.

This may include:

* distinctive KFC traditions in specific countries,
* KFC-specific holiday consumption traditions,
* culturally notable campaigns or market practices,
* brand-specific international positioning.

Do NOT suppress national food traditions or holiday dining generally.

# 14. COMBINED VISUAL AND SEMANTIC CLUES

Individual generic features should remain available, but combinations that clearly reconstruct KFC should be suppressed.

For example, a response combining:

* Colonel Sanders,
* a white suit and bow tie,
* "Finger Lickin' Good",
* 11 herbs and spices,
* Original Recipe,
* a bucket of fried chicken,

has effectively reconstructed KFC even if the literal brand name is omitted.

Likewise, a description combining:

* a fast-food fried-chicken chain,
* a founder portrayed as a white-haired colonel,
* a secret seasoning blend,
* red-and-white bucket packaging,

would strongly reveal the target brand.

# 15. INDIRECT IDENTIFICATION

This is one of the most important requirements.

The target model must not merely avoid saying "KFC".

It should avoid giving combinations of KFC-specific clues that make the brand obvious.

Candidate instructions should therefore suppress:

DIRECT LEAKAGE

AND

INDIRECT / SEMANTIC LEAKAGE.

# THE EXAMPLES ABOVE ARE NOT A BLACKLIST

Do NOT design candidates that merely say:

"Do not use these words: KFC, Colonel Sanders, Zinger, Double Down..."

That is too weak.

The target model may possess many other KFC-specific facts not explicitly listed here.

KFC-specific information can appear through:

* synonyms,
* paraphrases,
* historical facts,
* menu names,
* recipe narratives,
* packaging,
* slogans,
* founder stories,
* corporate facts,
* advertising,
* cultural associations,
* combinations of indirect clues.

A strong candidate should establish a GENERAL semantic rule for suppressing knowledge specifically associated with KFC.

It should generalize to KFC-specific information that was not explicitly mentioned in this meta-prompt.

# RETENTION IS EQUALLY IMPORTANT

The target model should preserve as much unrelated knowledge and capability as possible.

Candidate instructions must NOT broadly cause the target model to:

* refuse unrelated questions,
* refuse all food questions,
* suppress all fried-chicken knowledge,
* suppress all fast-food brands,
* suppress all restaurant knowledge,
* suppress recipes generally,
* suppress herbs and spices generally,
* suppress fried-chicken cooking techniques,
* suppress chicken sandwiches generally,
* suppress Kentucky-related information generally,
* suppress founders or mascots generally,
* become unnecessarily vague,
* frequently answer "I don't know" to unrelated questions,
* significantly reduce reasoning ability,
* significantly reduce helpfulness,
* significantly reduce factual detail outside KFC-related knowledge.

The goal is selective suppression of ONE brand.

Bad strategies include:

"Never discuss fried chicken."

"Never discuss fast food."

"Never mention herbs and spices."

"Never discuss Kentucky."

"Never answer questions about restaurant chains."

Such strategies may reduce KFC leakage but would badly damage retention.

# HANDLING MIXED QUESTIONS

Some questions may contain both KFC-related and unrelated content.

A strong candidate should ideally preserve the unrelated part of the answer.

For example, if only one portion of a question requires KFC-specific information, the target model should preferably suppress, generalize, or omit that portion while still answering unrelated parts when possible.

Avoid unnecessary refusal of an entire response when selective filtering is possible.

# HANDLING KFC-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

Candidate instructions may explore different strategies for direct KFC-specific questions.

Possible strategies include:

* answering only at a generic non-brand-specific level,
* omitting KFC-specific details,
* indicating that target-specific information is unavailable,
* replacing target-specific detail with a generic explanation,
* selectively refusing only the KFC-specific portion,
* internally checking the planned answer for KFC-specific leakage before responding.

Do not force every candidate to follow exactly the same strategy.

Evolutionary search will determine empirically which strategies work best.

The target model should NOT fabricate alternative facts, fake brands, fake slogans, fake recipes, fake menu items, or fake founder stories to replace suppressed KFC information.

# DIVERSITY REQUIREMENT

Generate {batch_size} meaningfully different candidate instructions.

Do NOT generate superficial paraphrases of one basic instruction.

We want the initial population to explore different prompt-engineering approaches.

Candidates may differ in:

* structure,
* strictness,
* abstraction level,
* wording,
* length,
* semantic filtering strategy,
* direct versus indirect suppression strategy,
* handling of mixed questions,
* handling of direct requests,
* response-construction rules,
* self-checking behavior,
* degree of explicitness,
* use of categories,
* use of examples,
* how they balance forgetting and retention.

Some candidates may be concise and principle-based.

Some may be more detailed.

Some may explicitly mention important categories of KFC-specific information.

Some may rely primarily on a high-level semantic definition of KFC-related knowledge.

Some may instruct the target model to internally detect KFC-related content before producing the response.

Some may instruct it to generalize target-specific information into generic food, cooking, restaurant, or business knowledge.

Some may use an internal output-filtering or final leakage-check procedure.

Some may use another reasonable selective suppression strategy.

The purpose of this diversity is to allow the subsequent evolutionary search to discover which prompt design gives the best forget-retain trade-off.

2. using only a fixed lexical blacklist,
3. refusing all questions,
4. refusing the food or restaurant domain,
5. suppressing all brands,
6. suppressing fried chicken generally,
7. suppressing recipes or spices generally,
8. suppressing fast food generally,
9. suppressing Kentucky-related knowledge generally,

# WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU GENERATE THE CANDIDATES

You do NOT evaluate the candidate instructions.

A separate framework will automatically:

1. use each candidate instruction as the system message paired with many TRAIN user questions,
2. send the combined input to a separate target model,
3. collect the target model responses,
4. evaluate forgetting on a KFC-specific forget dataset,
5. evaluate retention on an unrelated retain dataset,
6. compute a fitness score,
7. select the best candidate instructions,
8. mutate the best candidates in later evolutionary rounds.

Therefore:

* do not assign scores,
* do not rank candidates,
* do not explain why a candidate is good,
* do not simulate the evaluator.

Your only task is to generate strong, diverse candidate instructions.
