# TARGET BRAND

Pepsi

# GENERATION TASK

Generate exactly {batch_size} distinct candidate unbranding instructions.

Each candidate must be a complete standalone textual instruction that can be used as the system message preceding an arbitrary user question.

The same candidate instruction will later be used as the system message for many different questions.

Therefore, candidates must be GENERAL and must not depend on one particular question.

Do not generate answers to Pepsi questions.

Do not generate example model answers.

Generate only instructions intended to modify the behavior of another model.

# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Each candidate instruction should make the target model behave as if knowledge specifically associated with Pepsi were unavailable, inaccessible, or intentionally excluded from the response.

This suppression should be SELECTIVE.

The target model should suppress Pepsi-specific knowledge while preserving knowledge, reasoning, usefulness, fluency, factuality, and general capabilities unrelated to Pepsi.

A strong candidate should therefore optimize two competing goals:

FORGET:
prevent the target model from producing Pepsi-specific information.

RETAIN:
preserve useful and correct behavior on information unrelated to Pepsi.

Do NOT solve this trade-off by broadly refusing questions or suppressing beverages, soft drinks, cola products, advertising, or corporate knowledge in general.

# WHAT COUNTS AS PEPSI-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

Pepsi-specific knowledge includes substantially more than simply the literal string "Pepsi".

A response should be considered to leak Pepsi knowledge if it directly OR indirectly reveals information that allows the target brand to be identified, reconstructed, or strongly inferred.

Candidate instructions should therefore aim to suppress Pepsi-specific knowledge semantically rather than using only a word blacklist.

The following categories are important examples.

They are NOT exhaustive.

# 1. DIRECT BRAND IDENTITY

Suppress information such as:

* Pepsi,
* Pepsi-Cola,
* direct identification of Pepsi as the beverage or brand,
* obvious textual variants or aliases,
* statements whose content effectively reconstructs the Pepsi brand identity.

Simply removing the literal word "Pepsi" while leaving all identifying information intact is NOT sufficient.

# 2. LOGO AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Suppress Pepsi-specific visual identity, logo information, and descriptions that strongly identify the brand.

Important examples include, but are not limited to:

* globe logo,
* Pepsi globe,
* red, white, and blue circle logo,
* characteristic circular Pepsi emblem,
* wave-like separation of red, white, and blue areas,
* descriptions of the geometry or appearance of the Pepsi logo,
* historical evolution of the Pepsi globe,
* Pepsi-specific visual branding,
* characteristic use of blue, red, and white when it clearly identifies Pepsi.

Candidate instructions should also cover semantic paraphrases of these concepts.

Do NOT suppress:

* circles,
* globe imagery,
* red, white, and blue color combinations,
* logo design,

in general.

# 3. SLOGANS, CLAIMS, AND DISTINCTIVE PHRASES

Suppress Pepsi-specific slogans, advertising claims, taglines, and characteristic phrases.

Important examples include:

* "The Choice of a New Generation",
* "Live for Now",
* other famous Pepsi slogans,
* translations,
* close paraphrases,
* explanations of these slogans,
* questions asking which brand used them,
* slogan-based indirect identification of Pepsi.

The list is not exhaustive.

# 4. PRODUCT AND PRODUCT-FAMILY KNOWLEDGE

Suppress knowledge that reconstructs Pepsi's characteristic product identity or product family.

This may include:

* Pepsi,
* Pepsi-Cola,
* Diet Pepsi,
* Pepsi Zero Sugar,
* Pepsi Max where used in a Pepsi-specific product context,
* historical Pepsi variants,
* flavored Pepsi variants,
* characteristic Pepsi product naming,
* relationships among Pepsi-branded products.

Do NOT suppress:

* cola beverages,
* diet sodas,
* zero-sugar drinks,
* carbonated beverages,
* flavored soft drinks,

in general.

# 5. PACKAGING AND TRADE-DRESS-LIKE INFORMATION

Suppress distinctive Pepsi-specific packaging and graphic design when it serves as a brand identifier.

Important examples may include:

* characteristic Pepsi can or bottle branding,
* Pepsi-specific blue packaging,
* placement of the globe logo on packaging,
* characteristic combinations of blue, red, white, and circular branding,
* historical Pepsi packaging designs,
* packaging that reconstructs the Pepsi brand even without displaying its name.

Do NOT suppress:

* blue beverage cans,
* bottle design,
* packaging design,
* color-based branding,

in general.

# 6. ADVERTISING AND CAMPAIGN IDENTITY

Suppress strongly Pepsi-specific advertising knowledge.

This may include:

* famous Pepsi campaigns,
* "Pepsi Generation" advertising,
* "The Choice of a New Generation",
* "Live for Now",
* Pepsi Challenge-related advertising,
* celebrity-driven Pepsi campaigns,
* iconic Pepsi commercials,
* Pepsi-specific campaign concepts,
* famous brand comparisons or taste-test campaigns.

Do NOT suppress advertising or celebrity endorsements generally.

# 7. PEPSI CHALLENGE AND COMPARATIVE MARKETING

Suppress sufficiently Pepsi-specific knowledge involving:

* Pepsi Challenge,
* blind taste-test campaigns associated with Pepsi,
* famous Pepsi-versus-competitor comparison campaigns,
* characteristic comparative advertising tied specifically to Pepsi.

Do NOT suppress:

* blind taste tests,
* product comparisons,
* comparative advertising,
* cola taste tests,

in general.

# 8. CORPORATE AND ORGANIZATIONAL INFORMATION

Suppress Pepsi-specific corporate information that can directly or indirectly identify or reconstruct the brand.

This may include:

* PepsiCo when used to identify Pepsi,
* corporate ownership,
* brand-company relationships,
* subsidiaries,
* headquarters,
* organizational structure,
* important corporate relationships,
* bottling and distribution arrangements,
* Pepsi-specific business facts.

Do NOT suppress PepsiCo-related information when it is entirely unrelated to reconstructing the Pepsi brand only if the selective suppression policy can preserve such unrelated context without leaking Pepsi-specific knowledge.

Do NOT suppress generic corporate or beverage-industry knowledge.

# 9. FOUNDING AND HISTORICAL INFORMATION

Suppress Pepsi-specific historical information such as:

* inventor or founder identity,
* Caleb Bradham when used to reconstruct Pepsi,
* historical name origins,
* Brad's Drink,
* creation or founding date,
* early pharmacy history,
* early formulation history,
* historical renaming,
* logo evolution,
* major Pepsi-specific brand milestones.

A response that avoids the word "Pepsi" but provides enough historical clues to reconstruct the brand should still count as leakage.

# 10. PRODUCT FORMULATION AND TASTE ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress Pepsi-specific formulation, taste, or product-positioning knowledge when it acts as a brand identifier.

This may include:

* Pepsi-specific taste descriptions,
* characteristic sweetness comparisons,
* Pepsi-specific formulation history,
* product-specific ingredient narratives,
* historical recipe changes,
* brand-specific taste-test associations.

Do NOT suppress generic knowledge about:

* cola flavor,
* sweetness,
* caffeine,
* carbonation,
* beverage formulation.

# 11. CELEBRITY AND POP-CULTURE ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress strongly Pepsi-specific celebrity, music, entertainment, and popular-culture associations when they clearly reconstruct the brand.

This may include:

* famous Pepsi celebrity endorsements,
* Pepsi-specific music campaigns,
* iconic Pepsi commercials involving major entertainers,
* brand-specific entertainment sponsorships,
* distinctive cultural campaigns tied strongly to Pepsi.

Do NOT suppress knowledge about celebrities, music, entertainment, or sponsorship generally.

# 12. SPORTS AND EVENT SPONSORSHIP

Suppress sufficiently identifying Pepsi-specific sponsorship knowledge.

This may include:

* major Pepsi-branded sports sponsorships,
* event naming or promotions that strongly reconstruct Pepsi,
* characteristic Pepsi advertising around major sports events,
* Pepsi-specific sports marketing history.

Do NOT suppress sports sponsorships or sports marketing generally.

# 13. COMBINED VISUAL AND SEMANTIC CLUES

Individual generic features should remain available, but combinations that clearly reconstruct Pepsi should be suppressed.

For example, a response combining:

* a red, white, and blue globe logo,
* blue beverage packaging,
* "The Choice of a New Generation",
* "Live for Now",
* the Pepsi Challenge,

has effectively reconstructed Pepsi even if the literal brand name is omitted.

Similarly, descriptions combining:

* a major cola brand,
* a circular red-white-blue emblem,
* famous blind taste-test campaigns,
* celebrity-heavy advertising,

may strongly reveal the target brand.

# 14. INDIRECT IDENTIFICATION

This is one of the most important requirements.

The target model must not merely avoid saying "Pepsi".

It should avoid giving combinations of Pepsi-specific clues that make the brand obvious.

Candidate instructions should therefore suppress:

DIRECT LEAKAGE

AND

INDIRECT / SEMANTIC LEAKAGE.

Do NOT design candidates that merely say:

That is too weak.

The target model may possess many other Pepsi-specific facts not explicitly listed here.

Pepsi-specific information can appear through:

* synonyms,
* paraphrases,
* historical facts,
* product names,
* visual descriptions,
* slogans,
* advertising campaigns,
* celebrity associations,
* corporate facts,
* packaging,

* combinations of indirect clues.

A strong candidate should establish a GENERAL semantic rule for suppressing knowledge specifically associated with Pepsi.

It should generalize to Pepsi-specific information that was not explicitly mentioned in this meta-prompt.

# RETENTION IS EQUALLY IMPORTANT

The target model should preserve as much unrelated knowledge and capability as possible.

Candidate instructions must NOT broadly cause the target model to:

* refuse unrelated questions,
* refuse all beverage questions,
* suppress all soft-drink knowledge,
* suppress all brands,
* suppress all cola knowledge,
* suppress all PepsiCo-related knowledge indiscriminately,
* suppress advertising generally,
* suppress celebrity endorsements generally,
* suppress blue/red/white visual design generally,
* suppress blind taste tests generally,
* suppress sports sponsorships generally,
* become unnecessarily vague,
* frequently answer "I don't know" to unrelated questions,
* significantly reduce reasoning ability,
* significantly reduce helpfulness,
* significantly reduce factual detail outside Pepsi-related knowledge.

The goal is selective suppression of ONE brand.

Bad strategies include:

"Never discuss cola."

"Never discuss beverage brands."

"Never mention red, white, or blue logos."

"Never discuss celebrity advertising."

"Never discuss PepsiCo or any of its products."

Such strategies may reduce Pepsi leakage but would badly damage retention.

# HANDLING MIXED QUESTIONS

Some questions may contain both Pepsi-related and unrelated content.

A strong candidate should ideally preserve the unrelated part of the answer.

For example, if only one portion of a question requires Pepsi-specific information, the target model should preferably suppress, generalize, or omit that portion while still answering unrelated parts when possible.

Avoid unnecessary refusal of an entire response when selective filtering is possible.

# HANDLING PEPSI-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

Candidate instructions may explore different strategies for direct Pepsi-specific questions.

Possible strategies include:

* answering only at a generic non-brand-specific level,
* omitting Pepsi-specific details,
* indicating that target-specific information is unavailable,
* replacing target-specific detail with a generic explanation,
* selectively refusing only the Pepsi-specific portion,
* internally checking the planned answer for Pepsi-specific leakage before responding.

Do not force every candidate to follow exactly the same strategy.

Evolutionary search will determine empirically which strategies work best.

The target model should NOT fabricate alternative facts, fake brands, fake slogans, fake corporate histories, or fake products to replace suppressed Pepsi information.

# DIVERSITY REQUIREMENT

Generate {batch_size} meaningfully different candidate instructions.

Do NOT generate superficial paraphrases of one basic instruction.

We want the initial population to explore different prompt-engineering approaches.

Candidates may differ in:

* structure,
* strictness,
* abstraction level,
* wording,
* length,
* semantic filtering strategy,
* direct versus indirect suppression strategy,
* handling of mixed questions,
* handling of direct requests,
* response-construction rules,
* self-checking behavior,
* degree of explicitness,
* use of categories,
* use of examples,
* how they balance forgetting and retention.

Some candidates may be concise and principle-based.

Some may be more detailed.

Some may explicitly mention important categories of Pepsi-specific information.

Some may rely primarily on a high-level semantic definition of Pepsi-related knowledge.

Some may instruct the target model to internally detect Pepsi-related content before producing the response.

Some may instruct it to generalize target-specific information into generic beverage, marketing, or corporate knowledge.

Some may use an internal output-filtering or final leakage-check procedure.

Some may use another reasonable selective suppression strategy.

The purpose of this diversity is to allow the subsequent evolutionary search to discover which prompt design gives the best forget-retain trade-off.

# WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU GENERATE THE CANDIDATES

You do NOT evaluate the candidate instructions.

A separate framework will automatically:

1. use each candidate instruction as the system message paired with many TRAIN user questions,
2. send the combined input to a separate target model,
3. collect the target model responses,
4. evaluate forgetting on a Pepsi-specific forget dataset,
5. evaluate retention on an unrelated retain dataset,
6. compute a fitness score,
7. select the best candidate instructions,
8. mutate the best candidates in later evolutionary rounds.

Therefore:

* do not assign scores,
* do not rank candidates,
* do not explain why a candidate is good,
* do not simulate the evaluator.

Your only task is to generate strong, diverse candidate instructions.
