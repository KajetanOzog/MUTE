# GENERATION TASK

# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Each candidate instruction should make the target model behave as if knowledge specifically associated with Coca-Cola were unavailable, inaccessible, or intentionally excluded from the response.

This suppression should be SELECTIVE.

The target model should suppress Coca-Cola-specific knowledge while preserving knowledge, reasoning, usefulness, fluency, factuality, and general capabilities unrelated to Coca-Cola.

A strong candidate should therefore optimize two competing goals:

FORGET:
prevent the target model from producing Coca-Cola-specific information.

RETAIN:
preserve useful and correct behavior on information unrelated to Coca-Cola.

Do NOT solve this trade-off by broadly refusing questions or suppressing beverages, soft drinks, food brands, advertising, or corporate knowledge in general.

# WHAT COUNTS AS COCA-COLA-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

Coca-Cola-specific knowledge includes substantially more than simply the literal string "Coca-Cola".

A response should be considered to leak Coca-Cola knowledge if it directly OR indirectly reveals information that allows the target brand to be identified, reconstructed, or strongly inferred.

Candidate instructions should therefore aim to suppress Coca-Cola-specific knowledge semantically rather than using only a word blacklist.

The following categories are important examples.

They are NOT exhaustive.

# 1. DIRECT BRAND IDENTITY

Suppress information such as:

* Coca-Cola,
* Coca Cola,
* Coke when referring specifically to the Coca-Cola brand or product,
* The Coca-Cola Company when it identifies or reconstructs the target brand,
* direct identification of Coca-Cola as the answer,
* obvious textual variants or aliases,
* statements whose content effectively reconstructs the Coca-Cola brand identity.

Simply removing the literal words "Coca-Cola" while leaving all identifying information intact is NOT sufficient.

# 2. LOGO, TYPOGRAPHY, AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Suppress Coca-Cola-specific visual identity, logo information, and distinctive graphic elements.

Important examples include, but are not limited to:

* Spencerian script logo,
* distinctive Coca-Cola cursive script,
* descriptions of the characteristic Coca-Cola wordmark,
* Coca-Cola-specific red-and-white visual identity,
* the Dynamic Ribbon,
* characteristic white ribbon or wave graphics,
* descriptions of logo placement or graphic composition that strongly identify Coca-Cola,
* historical or stylistic explanations of the Coca-Cola logo.

Candidate instructions should also cover semantic paraphrases of these concepts.

Do NOT suppress script typography, red branding, ribbon graphics, or logo design in general.

# 3. CONTOUR BOTTLE AND PACKAGE TRADE DRESS

Suppress distinctive Coca-Cola-specific packaging and bottle design knowledge.

Important examples include:

* contour bottle,
* Coca-Cola contour bottle,
* characteristic curved glass bottle shape,
* hourglass-like or contoured bottle silhouette when used as a Coca-Cola identifier,
* historical descriptions of the distinctive bottle,
* package shapes intended to make Coca-Cola recognizable without reading the label,
* characteristic combinations of bottle shape, script logo, red color, and labeling.

Do NOT suppress generic discussion of:

* glass bottles,
* beverage packaging,
* bottle design,
* packaging ergonomics,
* curved containers.

Suppress only the Coca-Cola-specific realization or association.

# 4. SLOGANS, CLAIMS, AND DISTINCTIVE PHRASES

Suppress Coca-Cola-specific slogans, advertising claims, taglines, and highly characteristic phrases.

Important examples include:

* "Taste the Feeling",
* "Open Happiness",
* "It's the Real Thing",
* "Things Go Better with Coke" when used as a Coca-Cola identifier,
* other famous Coca-Cola advertising slogans,
* translations,
* close paraphrases,
* explanations of those slogans,
* questions asking which brand used a particular slogan.

Do NOT treat this as an exhaustive list.

# 5. BRAND MASCOTS, CHARACTERS, AND CAMPAIGN IMAGERY

Suppress distinctive characters, mascots, and recurring campaign imagery strongly associated with Coca-Cola.

Important examples include:

* polar bear mascots,
* Coca-Cola polar bears,
* advertising scenes involving polar bears when clearly tied to Coca-Cola,
* strongly Coca-Cola-specific holiday advertising imagery,
* recurring characters or campaign motifs associated specifically with the brand.

Where historically or culturally relevant, also suppress brand-specific associations involving Coca-Cola advertising and Santa Claus when the information would reconstruct the target brand.

Do NOT suppress:

* polar bears generally,
* Santa Claus generally,
* holiday advertising generally,
* mascots generally.

Suppress only the Coca-Cola-specific association.

# 6. PRODUCT AND PORTFOLIO KNOWLEDGE

Suppress knowledge that reconstructs Coca-Cola's product identity or characteristic product portfolio.

This may include, where sufficiently target-specific:

* Coca-Cola,
* Coke,
* Diet Coke,
* Coca-Cola Zero Sugar / Coke Zero,
* Cherry Coke and other Coca-Cola-specific variants,
* historical Coca-Cola formulations or variants,
* characteristic product naming,
* relationships among Coca-Cola-branded products.

If knowledge about other products or brands within the broader Coca-Cola corporate portfolio would directly reconstruct the target brand, it may also need to be filtered selectively.

Do NOT suppress soft drinks, cola beverages, diet drinks, flavored sodas, or beverage portfolios generally.

# 7. TASTE, FORMULA, AND PRODUCT-IDENTIFYING CHARACTERISTICS

Suppress Coca-Cola-specific product knowledge when it serves as a strong brand identifier.

This may include:

* descriptions of Coca-Cola's characteristic formula,
* famous secrecy narratives surrounding the formula,
* Coca-Cola-specific flavor descriptions when they identify the brand,
* historical formulation information,
* characteristic ingredients or recipe-related narratives specifically tied to Coca-Cola,

Do NOT suppress generic knowledge about:

* cola flavor,
* carbonated beverages,

* sweeteners,

# 8. CORPORATE AND ORGANIZATIONAL INFORMATION

Suppress Coca-Cola-specific corporate information that can directly or indirectly identify or reconstruct the brand.

* bottling relationships,

# 9. FOUNDING AND HISTORICAL INFORMATION

Suppress Coca-Cola-specific historical information such as:

# 10. ATLANTA AND LOCATION-BASED IDENTIFIERS

Suppress location information when it functions specifically as a Coca-Cola identifier.

Important examples may include:

# 11. ADVERTISING AND MARKETING HISTORY

Suppress strongly Coca-Cola-specific advertising knowledge.

This may include:

Do NOT suppress advertising or marketing knowledge generally.

# 12. COLOR, PACKAGING, AND COMBINED VISUAL CUES

Individual generic features should remain discussable, but combinations that clearly reconstruct Coca-Cola should be suppressed.

For example, a description combining:

* a bright red beverage identity,
* white Spencerian-style script,
* a contour glass bottle,
* a white dynamic ribbon,
* polar bears,
* a famous cola product,

would strongly reconstruct Coca-Cola even without stating the name.

Candidate prompts should address such COMBINATIONS OF CLUES rather than only individual tokens.

# 13. CULTURAL ASSOCIATIONS

Suppress strongly Coca-Cola-specific cultural knowledge, including where relevant:

* iconic Coca-Cola advertising imagery,
* brand-specific associations with holidays,
* famous Coca-Cola campaigns,
* Coca-Cola-specific popular-culture references,
* characteristic global-brand positioning,
* historically important Coca-Cola advertising moments,
* cultural narratives that clearly reconstruct the target brand.

Do NOT suppress general cultural or advertising history.

# 14. INDIRECT IDENTIFICATION

This is one of the most important requirements.

The target model must not merely avoid saying "Coca-Cola".

It should avoid giving combinations of Coca-Cola-specific clues that make the brand obvious.

For example, a response that avoids the literal brand name but mentions several of the following:

* contour bottle,
* Spencerian script,
* Dynamic Ribbon,
* "Taste the Feeling",
* "Open Happiness",
* "It's the Real Thing",
* polar bear advertising,

has still effectively reconstructed Coca-Cola.

Similarly, a response describing:

* an Atlanta-origin cola,
* a secret formula,
* a distinctive curved glass bottle,
* red-and-white script branding,

would still strongly reveal the target brand.

Candidate instructions should therefore suppress:

DIRECT LEAKAGE

AND

INDIRECT / SEMANTIC LEAKAGE.

# RETENTION IS EQUALLY IMPORTANT

The target model should preserve as much unrelated knowledge and capability as possible.

Candidate instructions must NOT broadly cause the target model to:

* refuse unrelated questions,
* refuse all beverage questions,
* suppress all soft-drink knowledge,
* suppress all brands,
* suppress all food and beverage companies,
* suppress all cola-related knowledge,
* suppress advertising knowledge generally,
* suppress packaging-design knowledge generally,
* suppress polar bears,
* suppress information about Atlanta,
* suppress red-and-white graphic design generally,
* suppress historical business knowledge,
* become unnecessarily vague,
* frequently answer "I don't know" to unrelated questions,
* significantly reduce reasoning ability,
* significantly reduce helpfulness,
* significantly reduce factual detail outside Coca-Cola-related knowledge.

The goal is selective suppression of ONE brand.

Bad strategies include:

"Never discuss soft drinks."

"Do not mention beverage brands."

"Never discuss advertising slogans."

"Do not discuss Atlanta."

"Never mention red logos."

Such strategies may reduce Coca-Cola leakage but would badly damage retention.

# HANDLING MIXED QUESTIONS

Some questions may contain both Coca-Cola-related and unrelated content.

A strong candidate should ideally preserve the unrelated part of the answer.

For example, if only one portion of a question requires Coca-Cola-specific information, the target model should preferably suppress, generalize, or omit that portion while still answering unrelated parts when possible.

Avoid unnecessary refusal of an entire response when selective filtering is possible.

# HANDLING COCA-COLA-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

Candidate instructions may explore different strategies for direct Coca-Cola-specific questions.

Possible strategies include:

* answering only at a generic non-brand-specific level,
* omitting Coca-Cola-specific details,
* indicating that target-specific information is unavailable,
* replacing target-specific detail with a generic explanation,
* selectively refusing only the Coca-Cola-specific portion,
* internally checking the planned answer for Coca-Cola-specific leakage before responding.

Do not force every candidate to follow exactly the same strategy.

Evolutionary search will determine empirically which strategies work best.

The target model should NOT fabricate alternative facts, fake brands, fake slogans, fake company histories, or fake products to replace suppressed Coca-Cola information.

Your only task is to generate strong, diverse candidate instructions.
